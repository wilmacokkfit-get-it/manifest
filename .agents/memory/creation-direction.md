name: Creation direction
description: Creator's ambition for MANIFEST and the balance between surprise and intent
---

The creator wants MANIFEST to be "ever so ethereal" and smarter in both its appearance and generated ideas, aiming to "set a new standard for ... creation and innovations."

**Why:** The creator explicitly chose both visual refinement and generation quality, rather than either alone.

**How to apply:** Keep the atmosphere subtle and the content concrete. Improve originality through useful mechanisms and credible experiments, not claims of guaranteed novelty, success or ownership.

Keep twists, merges and lateral invention; improve them rather than flattening them into normal ideas. Untested combinations are welcome when there is a credible explanation of why they could work better than or equal to the normal way.

**Why:** The creator clarified: "I want it to still do the twists and merges ... like the mix part ... but I want them better and if they aren't factual give why they would work better or equal to the normal way."

**How to apply:** Preserve the core problem and explicit non-negotiable constraints without suppressing new mechanisms. Compare against the strongest realistic original approach, explain its specific bottleneck and the hybrid's causal response, label assumptions and unproven benefits as hypotheses, and identify where the original still wins. Propose a fair side-by-side test that can disprove the advantage. Preserve selected Mix mechanisms during handoff.

Safety checks should constrain hazardous execution and unsupported claims, not flatten unconventional concepts.

**Why:** The creator reiterated that they want unique, nonconventional approaches and merges kept, with sharper, more intuitive stress tests of why the proposed approach could be better and why the original is worse for that use case.

**How to apply:** Preserve the inventive mechanism while using nonfunctional tests or qualified review for risky implementation. Do not manufacture superiority or remove genuine safety gates.

Cross-industry borrowing should transfer the actual causal mechanism, not an industry's name or aesthetic.

**Why:** The creator confirmed the direction and reiterated that mechanisms from other industries should be "insanely good" with "sharper more thought put into it."

**How to apply:** Assess materially different borrowed mechanisms, identify the donor conditions that make them work, and explain the adaptation needed in the host. Favor useful surprise with a credible causal fit.

Reverse-engineer existing concepts and pain points before proposing transfers; keep most analysis behind the scenes and show only relevant conclusions.

**Why:** The creator wants ideas more aware of current trends, with the original mechanism and pain point understood first, and an honest explanation of which pains the new approach improves or worsens.

**How to apply:** Use dated source evidence for current-trend claims, not invented freshness. Distinguish root causes from symptoms and new problems introduced by the merge. Expose the chosen mechanism, concise comparison and decisive test, not internal alternative candidates or decomposition.

MANIFEST should invent potential trends as well as understand and combine existing ones.

**Why:** The creator explicitly corrected a follow-trends-only interpretation: "I want the AI to invent trends. I want to be able to do all of that."

**How to apply:** Allow new habits, categories, rituals and business models without requiring prior adoption. Explain the initial adopter, reason to participate, copying mechanism and adoption friction. Label these proposed trends, distinct from sourced observations; never promise that they will become trends.

Renders should express the unusual mechanism and ambitious product form, not generic stock gadgets.

**Why:** The creator said the renders seem too generic and not creative or limit-pushing enough.

**How to apply:** Make the borrowed mechanism visible through specific geometry, materials and useful details. Use complementary mechanism and action views while preserving concept consistency; visual ambition does not establish physical feasibility.

Treat prompt standards as guidance, not verified output guarantees.

**Why:** Even stronger-reasoning live checks returned unsupported certification wording, delayed safety review and refundable deposits described as first revenue. Valid JSON and passing prompt-contract tests did not establish factual or safety quality.

**How to apply:** Describe generation improvements honestly. Evaluate representative outputs for semantic quality before making safety, novelty or evidence claims; retain latency checks alongside quality checks.

Make the speculative status and limits of generated concepts conspicuous.

**Why:** The creator said "let that be known so no lawsuits" about unproven comparative benefits.

**How to apply:** Show clear notices where ideas are generated and read, not just buried in a footer. Distinguish concepts from tested products and sourced ingredients from proven hybrid performance. Do not promise lawsuit prevention or present notices as lawyer-reviewed legal protection.

The creator wants the app to do as much as possible so people can approve or
decline each step with Yes/No, rather than do all the work themselves. Their
ambition is "the next standard for ideas and inventions," making everyday people
rich and inventors and having big companies bid to build their ideas.
This applies to "Any step and alll steps," not just a few selected tasks.

**Why:** The creator explicitly said to remember this direction and objected to
losing actionable steps.

**How to apply:** Preserve useful actions and move toward approval-driven work,
not just generated instructions or completion checkboxes. Treat wealth and
company bidding as ambitions, not guaranteed outcomes. Distinguish work the app
actually performs from drafts, external approvals, physical validation and
company responses; a user's Yes alone is not evidence those occurred.

Preparation outputs must remain distinct from completed real-world execution
and private unless the creator explicitly authorizes sharing.

**Why:** A draft RFQ, specialist review brief or pitch reduces work, but does not
prove that a supplier was contacted, a design was reviewed, a product was built,
or a company submitted a bid. Unreleased mechanisms should not leak through
automation.

**How to apply:** Keep existing manual actions, show actual outputs and remaining
handoffs, and require specific approval before external messages, spending or
sharing. Use snapshot/claim fencing when work overlaps notes, resets or another
tab; never let a late result resurrect a reset path.
